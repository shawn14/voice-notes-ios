The brief

Today, EEON's home shows you who you said you were. It should show you what you're doing.

The current home layout is locked in at the moment you tune EEON. State your purpose once, and the LLM picks 4–6 sections from a fixed catalog of 17. After that — even as you switch between client calls, deep research, and a shipping sprint — the same layout stares back at you for weeks.

This proposal threads a third path between fully manual ("re-tune yourself constantly") and fully silent ("the layout moves and you don't know why"). It borrows from Spotify Daylist, Linear Insights, and Superhuman: silent rearrangement that announces what it did. The persona layout you tuned remains the base. A small overlay re-orders middle sections based on a 14-day FocusVector. The chip at the top says exactly what mode it inferred.

01

Today's 3 never moves.

Same position, same component, every day, every mode. The daily-intentions ritual lives at index 0 by hard rule. Capture button anchors the bottom. Both are immovable, regardless of what FocusVector says. Adaptation is allowed only in the middle of the page.

02

The chip is the tell.

"Tuned for you" gets replaced with a named mode — Build, Research, Client. Same place on the screen, same shape, same one-tap-to-explain affordance. The user doesn't have to wonder why their layout shifted: the chip already said.

03

Middle sections permute.

Build mode lifts Active Build and Open Decisions. Research lifts Inquiries and Patterns. Client lifts People and Arcs. The persona layout from Tune EEON is preserved as the base — adaptation is an overlay that re-orders, never replaces.

Tap the chip

Every adaptation answers why.

The chip opens a sheet showing the FocusVector that drove the rearrangement, the underlying signal, and a one-tap escape hatch. The user always sees the math, never just the result.

If the system is wrong, "Reset to my Tune" rolls back to the persona layout in one tap and freezes adaptation for 7 days. The layout never traps the user.

Anchored — never moves

The pinned set.

Five elements have hard positions. The user's muscle memory works because these never shift. Failure-mode literature on adaptive UIs names this as the load-bearing constraint.

  • captureHero · the mic button, always at the bottom
  • todayThree · daily-intentions ritual, always index 0
  • openDecisions · accountability floor, ≥ position 4
  • openThreads · loose-ends floor, ≥ position 4
  • recentNotes · always the tail

Variable — re-ordered by FocusVector

The adaptive middle.

The 12 remaining sections in the catalog get permuted by the dominant FocusVector axis. Hysteresis = 0.15 prevents flicker. Cold-start (< 20 notes) and gap (> 72h) freeze the overlay and render the persona layout raw.

  • priorityProjects · lifted in Build
  • ideaInbox · neutral
  • activeInquiries · lifted in Research
  • recurringPatterns · lifted in Research
  • clientRoster · lifted in Client
  • relationshipArcs · lifted in Client
